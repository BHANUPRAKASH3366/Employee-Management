Alert email — 5 template options

Each option shows the same real edit (VC_242's name changed from “Kiranmai Battu” to “Kiranmai Batti”), first as it appears in the inbox list, then opened. All are built the way email needs (tables + inline styles), so they look the same in Gmail, Outlook and on phones. Pick one — or mix parts (e.g. A's subject line with C's body).

Why it works

  • Subject and preview already say who and what, so many people won't need to open it.
  • Before is struck through in red and After is bold in green.
  • The unknown editor becomes a small tip instead of a loud “— not identified —”.

Watch out

  • With 10+ changes it gets long (one card per field).
Best for: single or small edits — the most common case.

Why it works

  • The banner colour can mean something: blue = edit, red = rows removed, green = employee added.
  • One row per employee, with each change as “old → new”, so it scales to 20–30 changes.

Watch out

  • Less “wow” than A for a single change.
Best for: a good all-rounder — clear for 1 change, still neat for many.

Why it works

  • HR thinks in people, not cells: each person gets one card with all of their changes.
  • The initials avatar and designation make it easy to tell who's who.
  • New employees and removed employees can be cards with a green or red edge.

Watch out

  • Takes a little more space for a single change.
Best for: bulk edits — e.g. someone updates 15 people's projects at once.
